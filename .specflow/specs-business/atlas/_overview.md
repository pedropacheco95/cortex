# Atlas — Overview

## What this is

The project's memory of why. The outcome is that decisions, the people involved, the domain's own language, and the source materials behind them are preserved and traceable — so the "why" survives even when the people who knew it move on.

## What it covers

**Outcomes written:**

- **A developer turns raw sources into durable project memory** — originals preserved untouched, extracted memory always traceable to its source, re-ingest refreshes instead of duplicating.

_Planned (not yet written):_

- **Decisions with their reasons** — not just what was chosen, but why, kept where it can be found.
- **The people behind the work** — who was involved, so context isn't lost when they move on.
- **The project's own vocabulary** — domain terms preserved with their meaning, so language stays shared.
- **Traceable source materials** — the briefs, notes, and references a decision rests on, kept connected to it.

## Why it's grouped this way

This group exists because the most expensive thing a project loses is its reasoning. Code shows what was done, but rarely why, who decided, or what it was responding to. Preserving that "why" in a traceable form means a future maintainer can understand intent instead of guessing. That durable institutional memory is the outcome that belongs here.

What deliberately does not belong here is the active enforcement of rules — that's a separate outcome about applying conventions, not remembering their origin. This group is the archive of intent and context, not the mechanism that polices behaviour.
