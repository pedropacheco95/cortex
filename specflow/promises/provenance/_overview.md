# Provenance — Overview

## What this is

Nobody takes a rule on faith. Ask "why does this rule exist" and the answer is one hop away — a captured document, a recorded decision, or a stated "someone authored this directly." Ask the reverse — "a source changed; what was built on it?" — and the complete list comes back in one query.

## What it covers

- **Every enforceable rule traces to the authority that justifies it** — origins are recorded on the artefact itself in one consistent form; origins that point at something the project holds must actually resolve; conversation-borne origins are honestly cited rather than pretended retrievable; and no recorded origin means "authored directly," a complete answer rather than a gap.

## Why it's grouped this way

This group exists because the moment a client renegotiates a requirement or a decision gets revisited, the dangerous failure is silent: rules and specs built on the old agreement keep being enforced as if nothing changed. A trail that runs both ways — from artefact back to authority, and from a changed authority forward to everything derived from it — turns that silent drift into a visible, reviewable list. It is one discipline spanning every kind of governed content, which is why it stands alone rather than living inside any single module's outcomes.

What deliberately does not belong here: grading how strongly a source justifies what cites it, or automatically rewriting anything when a source changes — surfacing is in scope, judging and editing stay human.
